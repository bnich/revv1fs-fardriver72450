# Assembly

## Hardware

| Where | Part | Note |
|---|---|---|
| Frame | **2 × M5** per bracket | length = your frame's needs **+ 2 mm** if the side panels are on |
| Frame | **M5 compression limiters**, 8.2 mm OD | ⚠️ **cut to 16 mm** = 10 mm wall + 6 mm standoff |
| Controller | **4 × M6 × 30** socket cap | |
| Controller | **M6 limiters**, 10 mm OD | cut to the 10 mm shelf |
| Controller | **4 × M6 nyloc** + washers | nut goes **on top of** the shelf |

⚠️ **The two sides use different fasteners. Do not mix them.**

## ⚠️ Limiters are mandatory

Every bolt hole takes a metal compression limiter so the nut clamps **metal to metal** down the whole
length and the plastic never sits in the squeeze path.

**Never thread a bolt into the plastic.** A thread cut in ASA under a hanging load creeps and backs
out. Heat-set brass inserts are an acceptable alternative on the controller side; a through-bolt with
a nyloc is stronger and cheaper.

If you skip the limiters, the joint will feel fine on the bench and loosen on the road.

## The controller bolt stack

The controller's own holes are **plain Ø7 through holes**, not threads, so the bolt is captured by
the bracket:

```
bolt head + washer  under the controller's plate
  ↑ through the controller's Ø7 hole
  ↑ through the metal limiter in the shelf
washer + NYLOC      on top of the shelf
```

⚠️ The shelf's hole is **10.2 mm** because that is the *pocket for a 10 mm limiter tube* — not a
10 mm bolt. The bolt inside it is M6.

## Order

1. **Gauge first** — confirm 187 mm on your frame before anything else.
2. **Frame limiters into both brackets**, then bracket to rail. Pads bear on the lugs; **the bracket
   should touch the frame nowhere else.** If it contacts the tube, stop — the pad height is wrong for
   your frame, not the bolts.
3. **Snug, do not torque yet.** The second frame hole is elongated along the rail so the pair can find
   the lugs.
4. **Controller up to the shelves**, four M6 × 30 through the limiters, nyloc on top.
5. **Now torque everything**, working across rather than around.
6. **Proof-load.** Hang on it. Shake it hard. Do this on the bench, not on the first ride.

## Before you ride

- ⚠️ **Check shock clearance through full travel.** The controller sits rearward specifically to clear
  the shock; confirm it on your bike rather than trusting the model.
- **Re-check every fastener after the first ride.** A paint witness line across each nut and the shelf
  makes movement visible at a glance.
- Check the phase and battery leads are not strained or rubbing at full suspension travel.
